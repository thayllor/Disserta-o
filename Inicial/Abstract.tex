\chapter*{Abstract}

Multiplayer digital games provide interactive environments that may contribute to the development of social skills. This study aimed to investigate the potential of digital games as tools for social skills training. To this end, literature reviews were conducted, followed by an experiment using the game Among’Us, involving 15 participants divided into an experimental group (n = 9) and a control group (n = 6). Social skills were assessed before and after the experiment using the Social Skills Inventory (IHS-Del-Prette), supplemented by questionnaires addressing participants' perceptions. Data were analyzed using the Wilcoxon and Mann–Whitney tests, with Holm correction. The results showed mean increases in three of the five factors assessed in the experimental group, although no statistically significant differences were found. Nevertheless, all participants in the experimental group reported perceived improvements in their social skills and opportunities to meet new people. It is concluded that, although the effectiveness of the intervention could not be established, the exploratory findings suggest the potential of commercial multiplayer games as environments for social interaction, highlighting the need for further studies with larger samples.
